Technical white paper · version 1.0

Exact-input governance for registered AI-agent actions.

An implementation-backed account of Maqam 0.3.3, anchored to immutable tag v0.3.3 and commit f43c2493084f8a6c8c755a50a3d9feb38d72ebcc.

Publication statusThis open-access, project-authored technical report is archived at DOI 10.5281/zenodo.21851251 under CC BY 4.0. It has not undergone independent peer review, formal verification, penetration testing, or independent security certification.

What the paper establishes

The report traces Maqam's registered call path from policy evaluation through canonical input snapshotting, exact run/tool/input approval, one-use consumption, handler dispatch, execution receipts, and explicit evidence links. Each claim is scoped to the tagged implementation or a named historical evidence artifact.

What it does not claim

Maqam does not govern direct calls that bypass its gateway. It does not authenticate reviewers, replace an operating-system sandbox, make injected drivers trustworthy, provide distributed durability, or prove that evidence-linked claims are true.

Measurement boundary

Version 0.3.3 does not claim a new MGES measurement. The paper preserves the latest relevant historical measured-source result under its 0.3.1 source identity: 14 of 14 project-defined conformance fixtures passed, and a narrow in-process governed-call fixture recorded a median of 129.849 microseconds per call on its disclosed environment.

That fixture excludes model inference, network and filesystem I/O, durable storage, browser behavior, human review, process startup, and concurrent load. It is not a competitor benchmark, security score, production capacity result, certification, or SLA.
